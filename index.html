Reference

The five equations, and the projectile results that follow from them. All of it assumes constant acceleration in a straight line.

The model this site uses

Everything here is the idealised A-level model. That is deliberate — it is the model your specification assumes, and it is what makes the equations exact rather than approximate.

  • No air resistance and no wind
  • Gravity is uniform and vertical
  • The projectile is a point mass and does not spin
  • The ground is flat

The five equations

Each one leaves out exactly one of the five quantities. That omission is how you choose: pick the equation missing the thing you neither know nor need.

Projectile results

A projectile is just two SUVAT problems running at once: horizontal with a = 0, vertical with a = −g. They share only one thing — the time.

Resolving the launch

Split the launch velocity into components before anything else.

uₓ = u cos θ
u_y = u sin θ

Time of flight

From the vertical equation with s = −h. On level ground this halves to 2u sin θ ⁄ g.

t = (u sin θ + √(u²sin²θ + 2gh)) / g

Greatest height

At the top v_y = 0, so use v² = u² + 2as vertically.

H = h + u²sin²θ / 2g

Range

Horizontally there is no acceleration, so distance is simply speed × time.

R = u cos θ × t

Maximum range

From level ground, 45° is optimal. Launched from a height, the best angle drops below 45°.

R_max at θ = 45° (h = 0)

Complementary angles

On level ground θ and 90° − θ give the same range — the higher one just takes longer.

R(θ) = R(90° − θ)

Reading the graphs

Gradient

The gradient of a displacement–time graph is velocity. The gradient of a velocity–time graph is acceleration — constant here, so v–t is always a straight line.

Area

The area under a velocity–time graph is displacement; the area under acceleration–time is the change in velocity. Both are shaded live in the playground.

Why vₓ is flat

With no air resistance nothing acts horizontally, so aₓ = 0 and the horizontal velocity never changes. The equal-Δt marks make this visible: identical horizontal gaps, all the way.
